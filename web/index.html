        What am I looking at?

        The neck runs left to right, nut on the left. The thickest string - the low E in standard tuning - is the row at the bottom, exactly as it is when you look down at the instrument in your lap.

        Pick a chord on the left and the first shape appears under your hand. Try another voicing walks along the neck rather than nudging one finger: the second answer is a different place to play the same chord, not the same place with a string muted.

        On the dots switches what is written on them: which note of the chord each one is, or which finger goes on it. The colours do not change with it - red is the root whichever number is showing - so a barre chord in fingers still tells you that the two notes under your index finger are the root and the fifth.

        root third fifth everything else

        Twelve frets and then it repeats: the twelfth fret of any string is the open string an octave up. Nearly all of learning the neck is learning the first twelve on the two thickest strings - barre chords are named after their root, and that root is nearly always on one of those two.

        The quiz asks about the places you have got wrong more often than the places you have not, so a run gets harder in exactly the way it should - and it remembers between visits, so a fret you missed last week comes back this week. Show what you know paints the neck with that record: green where you have been right and it is recent, red where you have not.

        The record lives in this browser and goes no further. Clearing your site data clears it, and so does the button under the map.
